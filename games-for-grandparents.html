Games for Grandparents

Solitaire, Word Search and Sudoku for iPhone and iPad, rebuilt for people in their seventies and eighties. Everything is big. Nothing nags. Every Solitaire deal can be won, every hint explains itself, and there are no adverts anywhere in it.

Three Games

Solitaire

Klondike

Draw one card or three. Deals are not random: each one has been proved winnable before it is dealt, and if you get stuck the game will show you the next move of that winning line. Tap a card to send it where it belongs, or turn on "Choose where cards go" and place it yourself.

Word Search

25 themes

The county fair, Sunday drives, crooners and big bands, the sewing basket, church bazaars. Gentle puzzles run across and down only. Found words are marked with a colour and a pattern, so they stay clear if colours are hard to tell apart. Tap the first letter, then the last.

Sudoku

900 puzzles

Graded by how you solve them, not by how many numbers are missing. Hints tell you why in plain English first: "Only a 5 can go in row 1, column 4." A wrong number is marked gently, after a pause. No strike count, no lockout, no game over.

No Ads. Ever.

No adverts. No cross-promotion, not even for our own apps. No popups, no rating prompts, no Game Center, no coins, no countdown timers and no notifications. The home screen quietly counts the days you have played in a row, and never tells you off for missing one.

That is the whole product, not a launch promise. Nothing in the app interrupts a game once it has started. The timer is off unless you ask for it. Menus do not move between versions, because an older player learns one layout and should get to keep it.

Made for older hands and eyes

  • Four sizes for text, and four for cards, letters and numbers. Set it once and every game follows — including word lists and Sudoku pencil marks.
  • Tap to pick up, tap to put down. Dragging works, but no move ever requires it.
  • One giant Undo, always on screen, unlimited and free. Hints are free and unlimited too.
  • Warm cream paper instead of glaring white, plus a high-contrast mode.
  • Shapes as well as colours, for colour blindness. A left-handed layout. A "less movement" setting.
  • Full VoiceOver support: every card, letter and square can be read aloud, and every gesture has a spoken equivalent.
  • Sound never takes over the audio system, so a Bluetooth hearing aid does not drop its volume when a card lands.
  • Your game saves itself after every move. Close it mid-hand and come back tomorrow.

Requirements

iPhones and iPads from 2018 onwards, running iOS 17 or later. There are no accounts and no internet connection is needed to play.

The Family Pass

Games for Grandparents needs a Family Pass to start a new game. One pass covers up to six people in an Apple Family, so a son or daughter can subscribe from their own iPhone or iPad and their parent never has to enter a card.

What it costs

$4.99 a month, or $29.99 a year, in the United States. The App Store shows the price in your own currency. There is no free trial, and there is no other purchase in the app of any kind.

When you are asked

Once, calmly, when you start a new game. Never on opening the app, never in the middle of a hand, and never as a badge or a banner. A game already in progress stays playable forever, with or without a pass — nobody loses a hand they have started.

The screen has a full-width "Not now" button rather than a small cross, and it puts you back exactly where you were.

Cancelling

The subscription renews automatically until it is cancelled. Cancel any time in Settings on your device: tap your name, then Subscriptions. Cancelling at least 24 hours before the period ends stops the next charge.

For Families

The person who plays this and the person who pays for it are usually not the same person, and often not in the same house. Apple Family Sharing is built for exactly that, and the Family Pass uses it.

Buying it for a parent or grandparent

  1. On your own iPhone or iPad, open Settings, tap your name, then Family. If you have not set up a family yet, follow the steps to create one and invite them.
  2. Make sure Purchase Sharing is turned on for the family, and that you are the family organiser or a parent/guardian.
  3. Install Games for Grandparents on your own device, start any game, and buy the Family Pass when the pass screen appears.
  4. Ask them to install the app on their iPad or iPhone, signed in with their own Apple Account — the one that is in your family.
  5. They open a game. It just starts. No purchase, no sign-in, no code to type.

If the games are still asking them to subscribe

Have them open the app, tap Settings: text size and more, and then Restore purchases in the Family Pass section. That asks the App Store to check again. It works over Wi-Fi with no password.

If it still says no pass was found, check three things on their device: they are signed in to the right Apple Account, that account is a member of your family, and Purchase Sharing is on in your family settings.

Asking someone else to buy it

If it is the grandparent holding the iPad, the pass screen has a button reading "Ask someone to get it for me". It opens Messages or Mail with the explanation already written, so they only have to choose who to send it to.

Ask to Buy

On a child account with Ask to Buy switched on, the purchase waits for the organiser's approval, and the app says so. The games open once it is approved.

Support

Need help? Email sober@duck.com with the device (iPad or iPhone), the iOS version, and what you were trying to do. A photo of the screen helps more than a description.

Common Questions

Everything is too small. What do I change?

On the home screen, tap Settings: text size and more. The top of that screen has two rows of Smaller and Bigger buttons: one for text, one for cards, letters and numbers. A preview above them shows the change as you make it. There are four sizes, and the largest is very large indeed.

Are there really no adverts?

None. Not between games, not at the bottom of the screen, not as a reward for a hint, and not for our own other apps. There is also no Game Center, no leaderboards, no coins and no notifications.

Is the Solitaire deal I am on actually winnable?

Yes. Every deal comes from a list of positions that were solved in advance. The app does not announce it; it simply deals them, the way a pack of cards would if it were on your side. If you get stuck, the hint button says Show me while you are still on the winning line, and plays the next move of it.

I lost my game when I closed the app.

You should not have. Every game saves itself after every move, and the home screen offers a Continue button for the last one you played. If a game really has gone missing, please email us with what you were playing.

I already paid, but it is asking me to subscribe.

Open Settings: text size and more in the app, then tap Restore purchases in the Family Pass section. If it was bought by someone else in your Apple Family, check the For Families section above.

How do I cancel?

In Settings on your device: tap your name, then Subscriptions, then Games for Grandparents, then Cancel. It is Apple's screen, not ours, and we cannot cancel it for you. Games you have already started stay playable.

Does it need the internet?

No. All the puzzles, word lists and deals are inside the app. It works with the iPad in airplane mode. The internet is only needed to buy or restore the Family Pass.

Can a hint run out?

No. Hints and Undo are unlimited and free in all three games, forever. There is nothing to watch, unlock or spend.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 8, 2026

This privacy policy describes how 47 Summers LLC ("we", "us") handles information in connection with Games for Grandparents.

Summary

Games for Grandparents does not collect any information about you. There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no third-party code in the app. It does not need an internet connection to play. Your games and settings stay on your own device.

What We Collect

Nothing.

The app has no sign-in, no email capture and no analytics. It makes no network requests of its own — there is no code in it that can send anything anywhere. Its App Store privacy declaration is "Data Not Collected", and the privacy manifest bundled inside the app lists no collected data types and no tracking domains.

We do not know that you installed it, how often you play, which games you play, or whether you finish them.

What The App Stores On Your Device

These stay on your iPhone or iPad. We never see them.

  • Your settings: text size, the size of cards, letters and numbers, high contrast, shapes as well as colours, less movement, left-handed layout, timer, sounds and vibration.
  • Your games in progress: the current Solitaire deal, Word Search grid and Sudoku puzzle, saved after every move so you can close the app mid-game and come back to it.
  • How you like each game set up: draw one or draw three, table colour, card back, Sudoku notes preferences, and the last difficulty you chose.
  • A count of games you have finished, used only to say "that's your twelfth win" when you win. It is never sent anywhere and never reset.
  • One marker in the device keychain recording when the app was first opened on this Apple Account. It contains no personal information.

How This Information Is Used

Only to run the app on your device: to draw the screen at the size you chose, to put you back where you left off, and to say something kind when you win. It is not used for advertising, profiling or recommendations, because none of it ever leaves the device.

Sharing

We do not share, sell, rent or disclose personal information, because we do not have any.

The app contains no advertising networks, no analytics services, no social media kits and no other third-party software of any kind.

Payments

The Family Pass subscription is bought, renewed, shared and cancelled entirely through Apple. Apple handles the payment; we never see your card details, your billing address, your Apple Account email or your name.

The app asks Apple two things: what the subscription costs in your currency, and whether this Apple Account is entitled to it. Apple's own privacy policy governs that exchange. If you turn on Family Sharing, Apple tells the app that a pass covering this device exists — it does not tell us who bought it.

Tracking

The app does not track you. It does not use the advertising identifier, it never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, and it does not follow you across other apps or websites. There is nothing in it that could.

Children

The app is rated 4+ and is safe for a grandchild to play. It collects nothing from anyone, of any age, so there is nothing to collect from a child. It has no adverts, no chat, no links out to the open web and no user-generated content. The only purchase is the Family Pass, which goes through Apple's own purchase flow and, on a child account, through Ask to Buy.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13, or from anyone else.

Keeping And Deleting Your Information

We hold nothing, so there is nothing for us to keep or delete.

Everything the app has saved lives on your device. Deleting the app from your iPhone or iPad removes all of it: your settings, your saved games and your win counts. There is no cloud copy and no way to restore it afterwards.

To manage or cancel the Family Pass, open Settings on your device, tap your name, then Subscriptions. That is Apple's record, not ours.

Changes To This Policy

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new date at the top. Substantial changes will also be described in the app's release notes on the App Store. The policy that applies is the one published here.
